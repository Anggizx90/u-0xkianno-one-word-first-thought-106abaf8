# Choosing the word

The judge (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, briefed as a word-loving lexicographer) compares
two words at a time and weighs, most important first:

1. Exactly one word, the first word that comes to mind.
2. **Surprise** — a word a reader would not have guessed, not the safe one.
3. **Vividness** — a clear image or feeling in a few letters.
4. **Wit** — a spark of wit, sound or double meaning rewarding a second look.
5. **Chosen, not generic** — not filler like `thing` or `stuff`.

Length for its own sake does not count. Every candidate below is one word, so
criterion 1 ties and the contest is decided by 2–5, scored 1–5 each
(S = surprise, V = vividness, W = wit/sound/double meaning, C = chosen).

## The field to beat

The modal entry in a contest like this is a "beautiful words" listicle pick:
`serendipity`, `sonder`, `ephemeral`, `mellifluous`, `apricity`, `vellichor`,
`limerence` — and `petrichor` is already visible in the field. Any word from
that shelf loses on surprise before the other criteria are even reached. The
winning word therefore has to come from somewhere else entirely: concrete,
funny, slightly startling — a scene or a feeling, not another pretty abstraction.

## Why defenestration wins

- **Surprise (5).** It is nowhere near the "beautiful words" shelf the field
  is stocked from. Nobody's safe guess for "first word that comes to mind"
  is a 14-letter Latinate noun about window-tossing.
- **Vividness (5).** The highest in the shortlist: a whole slapstick scene in
  one word — a person sailing out of a window. Instant, specific, cinematic.
  Only `collywobbles`, `murmuration` and `tsundoku` come close, and each of
  those loses on surprise.
- **Wit (5).** The joke is built into the word: a polysyllabic, formal Latin
  construction (`de-` + `fenestra`, window) for a crude physical act — the
  formality *is* the punchline. It also carries real history (the
  Defenestrations of Prague), which rewards the second look the judge is
  asked to reward.
- **Chosen (5).** It feels deliberately picked, not fished from a thesaurus:
  it is the canonical word people actually blurt in word games, so it still
  reads as a genuine first thought rather than a dictionary stunt — unlike
  `borborygmus` (a medical term) or `syzygy` (a Scrabble flex).

## Why the runners-up lose to it

- **`collywobbles` (18):** the strongest challenger — the stomach-butterfly
  feeling is universal and the wobble is audible in the word. But it is
  gentler and slightly twee; a window-toss beats butterflies on surprise and
  on the vividness of the scene.
- **`syzygy` (17):** the visual shock of three y's is real, and it is short
  (6 letters, a tie-break edge). But its image — planets aligning — is a
  technical term, not a scene; against `defenestration` it loses the
  vividness criterion by a wide margin, and the judge's own rule lets a
  later criterion decide when it differs by much more.
- **`borborygmus` (17):** it rumbles like the stomach it names, a direct hit
  on the sound criterion. But it is clinical and faintly gross, and it reads
  as a dictionary pull rather than a first thought — it loses on "chosen".

## The word

**defenestration** — 14 characters, one word, exactly what `main.ts` writes.
